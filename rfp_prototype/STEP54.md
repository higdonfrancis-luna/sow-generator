## What the partner sees

The note under each drafted answer, "Notes for you, not for the client", now opens with "Internal. Nothing in this box goes into the response, the Excel or your client's workbook, except the asks you leave ticked (all start ticked) and what you type. All of it goes into your checklist." and holds four lists (the rules are the third round's, below):

| List | What lands in it | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Adobe licenses this proposal includes (Adobe quotes them) | a line that names a license, offering, entitlement, edition, package, add-on or SKU near its start ("The paid Brand Visibility offering, not the free trial", "Coworker Chat license through the Adobe account team", "For CJA audiences in Target: a Real-Time CDP or Journey Optimizer entitlement ..."), "The Client has licensed X" from a template, and a matched line's "also needs" ("Also needs Real-Time CDP or Journey Optimizer, licensed separately.", "Also needs the CIF add-on."); a license for a product the RFP says the client runs is an ask instead (confirm the package with them) | `LICENSE` in the opening words (`licenseAt`), product not owned |
| Ask your client | everything the client supplies or confirms: access, people, fields, acceptances, a template sentence whose subject is the client, its people or its things (`CLIENT_ASK`, `CLIENT_DOES`, "... are available" in the opening clause), and a matched line's tier ("Your Customer Journey Analytics package: CJA Select or higher.") | everything not caught by the other three |
| Confirm with Adobe | what only Adobe does (provisioning, Adobe's onboarding or support team, Customer Care, a Support ticket, "Adobe enabling": `ADOBE_PERFORMS`), what only Adobe can confirm (a route Adobe does not document, Adobe's own statement, a written confirmation, the CSM or TAM: `ADOBE_DOES`), an instruction to confirm with Adobe wherever it sits in the sentence or in the SOW's wording of it (`ASK_ADOBE`), and the caution a client-voice card leaves out (step 53's `sowNote`) | `ADOBE_PERFORMS`, `ADOBE_DOES`, `ASK_ADOBE` |
| Assumptions behind this answer | scope statements and exclusions ("Pilot users are a training scope, not a software seat limit", "Magento Open Source is not supported"), statements about how a product or the service behaves ("Scheduled refresh on 75 to 150 audiences, depending on the CJA entitlement", "These hours cover ...", "Hours scale with ..."), the partner's own to-do ("Select each destination's documented identity types ..."), the connection's availability line, and an "A or B" need one of whose products the RFP says the client runs ("Per the RFP your client already runs Real-Time CDP, which this line needs (...)") | `ASSUMPTION`, `STATEMENT_STRONG`, `STATEMENT`, `TO_PARTNER`; a template sentence that is not an ask; no field |

There is no six-item cap any more (finding 6.2): bank 2.2 shows all nine of its prerequisites; before, three were shown nowhere. Every rule is tested on the sentence's opening clause without its parentheticals, so "(CJA's page requires it for the source)" does not make the paid offering a CJA license and "(Adobe says agents are not offered on AEM 6.5)" does not turn an ask into a confirmation. A template sentence that gives a license and the client's duties in one breath ("The Client has licensed Brand Concierge and supplies the approved content ...") is read as two, and so is a prerequisite that gives a license and Adobe's own work. The note speaks to the partner: "the Client" reads "your client" there, and the partner reads about itself as "you" ("These hours cover your work", "you confirm its date in week one", "Skills you build and test") (finding 6.4).

Every ask has a tick box, on by default, a badge, and an empty field whose placeholder is "What your client told you" (the old pre-filled "Will confirm with the client" looked like typed text):

- Ticked: badge "In the response". The item goes into the response under **What we would need from you**, in the client's voice ("Your ad admins approve access in each network"); where the connection has the SOW's own client wording for the prerequisite (`sowPrereqs`), that wording is used, matched to the partner's item by its words because the two lists are not always in the same order. The connections the lists draw on are the ones the approach names (the first four).
- Unticked: badge "Not in the response". The item stays on the checklist only.
- A note typed in the field: badge "Answered by your note below", the tick box greys out, and the item moves to **What you already have in place, as confirmed with your team**, led by the ask's subject ("CDN access logs: Akamai serves both sites; your IT team confirmed log forwarding can be enabled.", "AEM release 26309 for Fusion: We are on release 26400.", "alloy.js in the site repository (Web SDK route): ..."). The note passes the same voice rewrite and client scrub as the rest of the response, whatever the partner called the two parties ("The client’s IT team", "Our client", "THE CLIENT", "Client confirmed", "the customer", "gave the partner"). The field keeps focus while the response updates.
- Skipped question: every badge reads "Not in the response: the question is skipped", the internal line and the checklist say so, and nothing from the box reaches the response, the Excel or the workbook.

The response's order is Our approach, What we would do, After launch, What you already have in place, What we would need from you, Adobe sources. Ticks and notes are kept per question when the partner moves between questions.

After Edit (the partner saved their own words), the internal line says "You edited this answer, so ticks and notes here no longer change it; they go into your checklist only. Edit it again and type them in." (finding 6.5, option b), and each badge says whether the saved text still carries the ask: "In the edited answer" when the prefilled draft was kept, "Not in the edited answer" otherwise. The fields stay, so the checklist still records what the client said. A saved answer whose products are then taken out (so no draft is left) stays in view with Edit and Skip, since it still reaches the client; a pile change to Your firm and back clears the draft, the ticks, the notes and the product picks together.

## What the skeptics found, and what changed

Two rounds of skeptics on the built page (two per review finding) found gaps in the first build of this step, all closed: ticks and typed notes were stored by position, so after the partner took a product out or added one through the Drawn from chips they attached to a different ask (now keyed by the ask's own words, and a note whose ask is gone no longer reaches the client); a drafted answer with no connection took every template assumption as an ask, so scope statements ("The prompt count is the service scope") were ticked by default and reached the client (now only ask-shaped sentences are asks); when the answer named a second documented connection its prerequisites were never offered (now every connection the answer uses contributes its prerequisites and availability); a prerequisite whose SOW wording says "is confirmed with Adobe" reached the client (now sorted under Confirm with Adobe); an emptied edit printed the full draft again (now it leaves the answer out); after an edit the badges still said "In the response" (now "Not in the edited answer", in the checklist too); "we already license Brand Visibility and Customer Journey Analytics" marked only the first as owned (now both); a pair whose services are all optional lines drafted nothing (now it drafts from the first of them); "Is there anything you need from us ...?" was read as a yes/no capability question (now it lists the asks); a note typed about "the client" reached the client in those words (now "your"); and a license's "A or B" need counted as a license to sell even when the RFP said the client runs one of the two (now "Already in place per the RFP").

The third round (findings 6.3 to 6.7, A8 and both decisions, read on the page) found that the fixes above had gone too far or not far enough, all closed: the near-duplicate rule that folded "a repeat in other words" dropped documented prerequisites from every list (three of Target + Edge Delivery's seven; a trio's later connection lost items to an earlier one), so the note now folds exact repeats only; a license word anywhere in a sentence made it a license ("Legacy offer route: Target as the reporting source ...", "Scheduled refresh ..., depending on the CJA entitlement", "The work covers Coworker Chat in the Client's licensed ... applications; Coworker for teams ... is out of scope"), so a license line now names the license near its start and statements are sorted first; Adobe's own work ("Adobe Target Customer Care has enabled ...", "Adobe enabling the configured plugin", "CIF add-on provisioned by Adobe") reached the client as a need, so it is Confirm with Adobe; "A publicly reachable HTTPS Commerce GraphQL endpoint; private VPN or local connections are not supported" had become an assumption on its second clause, so the exclusion words are read on the opening clause only; template statements still became asks through single words ("These hours cover ...", "Hours scale with ...", "This service enables ...", "The source datasets don't need to be enabled", "Publishers and community editors retain all editorial decisions", "Brand Visibility runs the tracked prompts ... and the Client review"), so a template sentence is an ask only when the client, its people or its things are its subject; "Already in place per the RFP" asserted a paid tier or a sibling license the RFP never mentioned, so an owned product's license line is an ask and only an "A or B" alternative one of whose products the client runs is an assumption, in words that say so; the note still said "the partner", "Partner-built", "Credible Client evidence", "Client-designated", "Client feedback" and "Client expertise", and the on-screen cautions skipped the partner voice the checklist applied; a typed note with a curly apostrophe reached the client as "You's", and "Our client", "The CLIENT", a bare "Client confirmed", "the customer", "gave the partner", "we was", "its own" after a Client swap and "the Client review" (a noun) all came out wrong; a typed fact skipped the client scrub, so "Legacy" reached the client; the fact's lead came out as "and", "Adobe enabling the", "How an", a one-word "Access" or a lowercase "full URL field", so the lead is the noun phrase before the first verb or preposition, a label becomes a bracket, a leading clause moves behind the subject, one-word leads keep their phrase, and leads are capitalised; a skipped question's badges, box and checklist still said "In the response"; the internal line said "the downloads" while the checklist download carried the whole box, and "except what you tick" hid that every ask starts ticked; the edited-answer line showed on the capability box, and after a prefilled Edit every badge said "Not in the edited answer" though the saved text carried the needs; the rules-out and not-in-catalog lines were missing from the checklist; a question that draws more than four connections asked the client for prerequisites of connections the approach never named; the client scrub dropped any sentence with "card" ("test product cards and comparisons" became "We will cart, checkout ..."), so only the canvas phrases are dropped, and "the SOW identifies", "Do not assume" and "state whether" reached the client as written; "audiences built in Customer Journey Analytics" and "run in Adobe Target activities" marked the products owned; a question naming only the client's BI tool under a CJA heading drafted nothing; a saved answer disappeared behind "No match" when a product was taken out; and a pile change kept ticks and notes but not the draft.
