### A.4 Describe your go-live and cutover approach for the new AEM Sites website, including how you protect our search rankings.
[claude-opus-5-5; SI04]

- With about 2.4 million visits a month and a nine-month launch target, we treat cutover as a rehearsed event that protects your search history.

How we would do it
- We crawl your site, rank URLs by traffic and search visibility, and build a redirect map from every retired URL to its new AEM page, signed off by you.
- We work through Adobe's go-live checklist: production pipeline with functional and UI tests, Dispatcher validation, CDN, SSL, DNS, notification profiles and traffic filter rules.
- We run performance and security tests in the stage environment, which has the same sizing as production, then user acceptance testing on a test subdomain.
- We hold a content freeze for the final top-up, check migrated content with you and test every redirect automatically against the map.
- We cut over DNS against agreed rollback criteria, then recrawl and compare status codes, titles and redirects with the old site.

What you get
- URL inventory, signed redirect map, completed go-live checklist, stage test reports, cutover and rollback plan, post-launch crawl comparison.

Timeline and effort
- Rehearsal in month eight, cutover in month nine. Planning estimate: 80 hours for go-live checklist and cutover.

Risk we manage
- The main risk is lost rankings from missing redirects. A signed map, automated tests before cutover and a post-launch crawl contain it.
