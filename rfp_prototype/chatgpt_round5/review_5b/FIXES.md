## Group 1: the reader (items 1 to 4)

1. **Numbered chapter headings lose their section.** `chapters_kingsfold.pdf`: running header, "Page n of 3" footer, bold headings "1. Digital Experience", "2. Data and Analytics", "3. Implementation", "4. Vendor Profile", questions 1.1 to 4.3. You find all 13 questions but give every one the section "1. Digital Experience"; the base branch gave each its own. Cause found: in `pdfParts` the first numbered heading lands in the front matter (a text part) and sets the section; every later one takes the `title`/`numberedHeading` branch, is pushed `contextOnly`, and `scanContext` never calls `newSection`. Expected: each question carries its own chapter as its section, in the reader and in the Word proposal's grouping and matrix.

2. **A heading such as "Overview" swallows the questions under it.** `overview_heading_harrow_lake.docx`: a Heading 1 "Overview" followed by "1.1 Provide an overview of your firm and its Adobe practice." and "1.2 Describe two comparable credit union or banking implementations." Both vanish: not in the list, not in Not picked up. The base listed both. Cause found: `CONTEXT_HEADING` (and `CONTEXT_HEAD` and the `flushFront` pattern in `pdfParts`) marks the whole block context by its heading name, and the names listed are exactly the brief headings your fixtures use. Expected: a heading name never hides a numbered requirement; whatever is set aside always appears in Not picked up. Treat a block as context because of what it contains (no request, instruction wording), not because of its heading.

3. **A paragraph that starts with "A" plus a capital is taken for a section heading.** `a_paragraph_kingsfold.pdf`: after "1.3 Describe your migration approach for our current site." an indented, wrapped paragraph "A Drupal 9 site with about 3,000 pages and 40 content types runs our site today / and must be retired at launch." `sectionHeading = /^[A-Z]\.?\s+[A-Z]/` matches "A Drupal", closes 1.3 and sends the paragraph to Not picked up; the base kept it in 1.3. `LETTER_HEAD` in `startsItem` has the same risk for pasted text. Expected: the sentence stays in 1.3. A letter heading needs heading evidence (its own short line, larger or bold type, followed by numbered items, or a known section pattern), not just "letter, space, capital".

4. **"Must not exceed N words" is no longer applied.** Paste: "Request for Information\nResponses must not exceed 120 words.\n1. Describe how you would build our new website on AEM Sites with Edge Delivery Services.\n2. Describe how you would implement Customer Journey Analytics." Base: both answers capped at 120. Round 5: `limit` null, first answer 463 words. Cause found: `WORD_CAP` accepts only "not to exceed", and `limitOf` now returns null on `q.limit === null` before the older fallbacks (`limitIn`, `LIMIT_RES`). Also fails: "Each response must not exceed 50 words.", "Answers must not exceed 150 words." under a section heading, "each response must be not exceeding 200 words". `word_limits.txt` has more. Expected: every wording the base caught is still caught, and `null` means "no limit found anywhere", not "skip the fallbacks".

5. **Section names carry part of the limit instruction (Cedar Prairie).** Drop `sow/rfp_prototype/demo/Cedar_Prairie_Mutual_Insurance_Company_RFP.pdf`. Sections read "Section B. Website on AEM Sites with Edge Delivery Services Answer each requirement in this section in" (and C to G alike), and the Word proposal's headings and matrix print that. The limits (300, 250, 250, 150, 200, 250) are now right; keep them. Cause found: `headingInfo()` cuts the name at the `WORD_CAP` match, leaving the instruction's opening words. Expected: the name is the heading alone ("Section B. Website on AEM Sites with Edge Delivery Services").
