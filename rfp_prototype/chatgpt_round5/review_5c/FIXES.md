## Group 1: word limits (items 1 to 4)

Root cause found by the reviewers: in `instructionCaps`, `else if(st.section&&!st.inContext)st.sectionLimit=limit;` files a document-wide "each answer" limit as the limit of whatever section it sits under, the next heading resets it, `responseItem` marks every question `limitScoped`, and `limitOf` returns null before the base fallbacks (`limitIn` over skipped lines and paragraphs) run. Fix the rule, not the examples: a limit that says each/every/all answers, responses or questions, or is in an instructions or response-guidance part, is **document-wide** unless it says "in this section"; and `limitOf` must fall back to the base logic whenever the new logic finds nothing.

1. **Tallgrass demo loses 250.** `sow/rfp_prototype/demo/Tallgrass_Home_and_Garden_Inc_RFP.pdf`: "Keep each answer under 250 words unless the question asks for more." under "3. Questions for bidders". Base: 249 on all 20 questions. Now null, answers 280+ words.
2. **"Instructions to Respondents".** Paste: "Request for Information: Lakeshore Health Website\n\nSection 1. Instructions to Respondents\nAll responses must not exceed 90 words.\nSubmit your response by email to the procurement office.\n\nSection 2. Website Requirements\n2.1 Describe how you would build our new public website on AEM Sites with Edge Delivery Services.\n2.2 Explain how you would implement Customer Journey Analytics for our website." Base: 90. Now null, 354 words.
3. **Wordings the base caught.** Paste "Request for Information\n<line>\n1. Describe how you would build our new website on AEM Sites with Edge Delivery Services.\n2. Describe how you would implement Customer Journey Analytics." with each of: "Maximum 300 words per question.", "Proposals should not exceed 400 words per question.", "Your response to each question must not exceed 90 words.", "Respondents must not exceed 140 words per answer.", "Each question has a 250-word limit.", "The response to each item shall not exceed 230 words.", "The narrative for each response must be not exceeding 200 words." All give the number on base and null now; same under a "Section 1 - Website and analytics" heading and in a PDF ("Section 3 - Analytics" over "Maximum 150 words per answer."). A section limit must also win over a looser document limit, never the reverse.
4. **A lettered PDF heading followed by a line of text.** `lettered_limit_s1.pdf` (made by `make_lettered_pdfs.py`): Heading2 "A. Public Website", then "Responses in this section must not exceed 100 words.", then A.1 and A.2. Base: section "A. Public Website", limit 100, A.1 76 words. Now the heading and instruction become a To sort question "A", A.1 and A.2 have no section and no limit, A.1 is 354 words. The script's other variants ("Answers in this section must not exceed...", "Each..."), behave the same.

## Group 2: headings and sections (items 5 to 9)

5. **Section names keep instruction words.** Cedar Prairie's own wording is fixed, but other wordings leak: "Keep responses in this section under 300 words." gives "Section B. Website on AEM Sites with Edge Delivery Services Keep responses in this section"; likewise "Please limit each answer to a maximum of 250 words.", "Each reply should be no more than 150 words.", "Answers in this section must not exceed 250 words.". Expected: a section's name is its heading line only. Decide the name from the heading's own line (type size, bold, its own line in the PDF; the heading paragraph in Word), never by cutting a joined block at a limit match.
6. **Unnumbered bold headings in a PDF.** Headings "Submission Instructions", "Scope of Work", "Background" (bold 14pt) over questions 1. to 4.: every question gets "Submission Instructions", and "Scope of Work" and "Background" go to Not picked up. Expected: each heading starts a new section, whatever its name.
7. **Roman numeral chapters.** "I. Introduction", "II. Scope of Work", "III. Analytics Requirements", "IV. Supplier Information" over 1.1 to 4.2: no section and all four headings in Not picked up. Expected: each question carries its chapter.
8. **A context heading still hides unnumbered requests.** Paste: "Request for Information\n\nInstructions\nAll responses are due on 15 January.\nDescribe how you would build our site on AEM Sites with Edge Delivery Services.\n\nBackground\nWe run Sitecore today.\nExplain how you would implement Customer Journey Analytics." Base: 2 questions. Now 0. Also "Requirements\nResponses must not exceed 120 words.\nDescribe how you would build our site on AEM Sites with Edge Delivery Services." gives 0 (base 1). Expected: judge each sentence, not the start of a joined paragraph; a request sentence is a question wherever it sits; a context heading's scope ends at the next heading.
9. **A client that names itself in an exclusion.** A.2: "Northfield Water Authority will not license Adobe Target or Adobe Analytics in this program. Describe how you would test two versions of the Pay my bill page and report results within 30 days of launch." A.3: "We do not use Marketo Engage and will not license it. Explain how site forms would reach our Salesforce CRM." The exclusion applies to A.2 only, so A.3's download proposes Adobe Analytics. Expected: "[the client's name] will not license X in this program" is document-wide, like "We will not license X".

## Group 3: hours land where the work is described (items 10 to 12)

10. **Item 8 from 5b holds only while nothing is approved.** Briarhaven with every row marked Use this answer: Document versions (32 h) lands in B.1 while B.4 says "counted elsewhere"; Go-live checklist (80 h) in A.3 while F.2 says "counted elsewhere"; DAM onboarding in B.2; Form templates in C.2. Cause: `homeOf()` returns the first approved question, in RFI order, whose draft lists the service. Expected: approved or not, a service is counted in the question whose answer best describes it (the same rule as the draft state), and that answer shows it.
11. **Product support questions lose their hours.** `northfield60.pdf`, nothing approved: B.8 "Describe the monthly analytics support you would provide after launch." takes every product's monthly service (204 hours a month), while E.10 "Explain the monthly support you would provide for AEM Assets." says "Estimate in discovery" though AS06 fits exactly; C.10 (Real-Time CDP and Journey Optimizer support) and A.6 (page templates and components) likewise. Expected: a question that names a product gets that product's matching service first; a question that names none takes only what no named-product question claims.
12. **A blank service description in Briarhaven.** "User onboarding and training" (D.4) prints only a footnote number: removing the ruled-out "Adobe Analytics" sentence left nothing. Expected: when removing a ruled-out sentence leaves a description empty, use the service's name-based fallback text, never a bare number.
