# DeepSeek review (as pasted by Adam, Nov 9 2026)

**Verdict:** I could not run the page, and no screenshots or downloaded RFP outputs were attached; only the three JavaScript libraries were provided. So this is graded from the written behavior, not the actual UI. As described, the RFP Builder is a useful catalog-backed triage and first-draft tool, but it is not an RFP compliance system. It will under-extract real procurement artifacts, over-draft where the catalog has no proof, and silently drop mandatory client prerequisites. **Grade: C+ as an internal prototype; do not let partners submit from it without a human compliance pass.** The biggest risks are traceability, mandatory/desirable handling, compliance matrices, and source-to-claim honesty.

## A. Findings

1. **Critical — numbered requirements without question marks will be missed.** Trigger: `R1. The solution shall provide a unified customer profile across channels.` Expected: extract as a requirement, preserve `R1`, tag mandatory if indicated, map to RTCDP/CJA, and mark "Draft available" or "No catalog source." Likely current behavior: only question-mark or interrogative sentences are treated as questions.
2. **Critical — tables with a `Response` column will break or be flattened.** Trigger: `| ID | Requirement | Response |` / `| R-01 | The system must support real-time activation. | |`. Expected: extract `R-01`, the requirement text, and an empty response cell mapped to the answer; export in the same table shape. Likely current behavior: row treated as one blob, `Response` included as text, or table ignored.
3. **Critical — Yes/No/Partial compliance matrices get prose instead of a compliance token.** Trigger: `R-12: The bidder shall confirm compliance with Section 4.2 (Yes/No/Partial).` Expected: a direct `Comply` / `Partially comply` / `Non-comply` answer plus evidence and source; no "Our approach" essay in the compliance cell. Current design drafts narrative answers, which is the wrong format for procurement.
4. **High — sub-questions a, b, c are likely collapsed into one parent.** Trigger: `4.1 Describe: a) data ingestion; b) identity resolution; c) activation.` Expected: three child requirements with their own status and traceability, or one parent with three tracked sub-answers. Likely current behavior: one question found, b and c lost.
5. **High — mandatory versus desirable tags are not reliably preserved.** Trigger: `M1 (Mandatory) The solution must…` and `D1 (Desirable) The solution should…`. Expected: mandatory flag, separate filtering, and export/blocking behavior if a mandatory item is unanswered. Current behavior likely treats both as ordinary questions.
6. **High — questions split across cells, pages, or headers will be lost or malformed.** Trigger: PDF text extraction: `The solution must support` / `[page header]` / `real-time activation across channels.` Or a table cell split across a page break. Expected: join by requirement ID/section and strip repeated headers/footers. Likely current behavior: two fragments, one of which is unanswerable.
7. **High — appendices and security questionnaires will be under-classified.** Trigger: `Appendix C – Security Questionnaire` followed by 80 rows of "Do you support…?" Expected: include appendices, classify as security/compliance, and route to the planned knowledge base or "Your firm," not draft product marketing. Current design focuses on the main RFP body and catalog matches.
8. **High — CSV/Excel quoted multiline cells will break on naive splitting.** Trigger: CSV row: `"R-1","The system must\nsupport real-time activation",""`. Expected: parse the quoted newline as one requirement. Also, the supplied libraries include PDF.js and Mammoth but no XLSX reader; I cannot verify Excel input from the attachments. If Excel is claimed, verify the parser.
9. **Critical — overconfident answers outside the catalog.** Trigger: `Does Real-Time CDP support data residency in Australia?` Or `Is AEM Sites WCAG 2.2 AA compliant?` Or `What is the uptime SLA for Adobe Commerce?` Expected: "Not in the current catalog; no claim; route to the planned static knowledge base / client / Adobe." Current catalog has 23 products and connections, not hosting, certifications, accessibility, uptime, retention, or license packages. A static keyword match must not turn an Adobe product page into a compliance claim.
10. **Critical — multi-product composition is treated as if pairwise connections prove an end-to-end solution.** Trigger: `Explain how AEM Sites, Target, CJA and RTCDP work together for a single customer view.` The catalog has 75 pairwise connections, not a documented four-way architecture. Expected: "Partial coverage; pairwise sources; no end-to-end proof" or split into pairwise claims with explicit gaps. Likely current behavior: concatenate pairwise text and imply a proven end-to-end design.
11. **High — source-to-claim mismatch.** Trigger: `How many weeks to implement AEM Sites?` Expected: no duration claim unless the partner enters an estimate; Adobe product pages do not prove implementation effort. Current service lines carry Adobe sources, but a source that proves a feature does not prove the partner's effort, staffing, or timeline. A procurement reader will treat the source list as stronger evidence than it is.
12. **High — blank "Ask your client for" fields silently disappear from the client response.** Trigger: prerequisite `Experience Platform user` left blank. Expected: the response marks an unresolved client prerequisite and the export flags it; the separate checklist is not enough because the client-facing response can look complete while a mandatory prerequisite is missing. Current behavior: blank fields stay out of the response and only appear on the checklist.
13. **High — wrong actor/voice.** Trigger: `The bidder shall provide a certified AEM project manager and three public-sector references.` Expected: "Your firm" / partner-written, because this is staffing and references. Likely current behavior: the word "AEM" pulls it into Adobe services and drafts "we would…" about the product. Also watch `Adobe provides 99.9% uptime`: the partner must not imply it warrants Adobe's SLA.
14. **Medium/High — "Our approach" can be filler that sounds sourced but is not specific.** Trigger: any question with a partial product-name match but no exact connection. Expected: each claim names the product, the connection, the client prerequisite, and the exact Adobe page; if not, say "catalog coverage partial." Current design rewards drafting over honest coverage.
15. **Medium — Excel `Status` is not compliance-grade.** Trigger: download the workbook. Expected columns/status such as `Requirement ID`, `Section`, `Mandatory/Desirable`, `Compliance`, `Owner`, `Evidence/Source`, `Unanswered — mandatory`. Current `Response` sheet with `Number, Question, Response, Status` is a draft tracker, not a compliance matrix.
16. **Medium — the internal boxed note can be misread as client-facing.** Trigger: first-time partner sees `Part of this proposal` and `Ask your client for` next to the drafted answer. Expected: explicit `Internal — excluded from client downloads` banner and a visible exclusion rule. The current wording is not strong enough for a partner under deadline.
17. **Medium — "What you have in place" is ambiguous about who typed the fact.** Trigger: partner types `Experience Platform user` as `Yes`; the client response says `You have an Experience Platform user`. Expected: make clear these are client-confirmed prerequisites, not partner assumptions, and validate required fields before export.

## B. What is missing for a systems integrator

1. **Requirement traceability and compliance matrix — Rank 1.** Who: bid manager/proposal lead. Saves: manual Excel reconciliation, missed mandatory items, re-keying IDs. Browser: yes for extraction, mapping, and Excel export; AI helps extraction but is not required for basic ID preservation. Size: L. This is the single biggest gap: every requirement needs ID, section, mandatory/desirable, compliance status, owner, evidence, and source.
2. **Reusable approved answer library with source and reuse tracking — Rank 2.** Who: solution architects and partner SMEs. Saves: rewriting the same Adobe-product answer across RFPs; improves consistency. Browser: possible in IndexedDB/localStorage for one user; team sharing needs a server. AI: semantic search is useful but must cite. Size: M browser-only, L with server.
3. **Team review and sign-off workflow — Rank 3.** Who: bid manager, legal, security, delivery lead. Saves: email/Word churn and "who approved this?" risk. Browser-only: limited comments/versioning; multi-user review and audit trail need a server. AI: not essential. Size: L.
4. **Structured static knowledge base for hosting, security certifications, accessibility, uptime, support terms, data retention, and license packages — Rank 4.** Who: architects, security responders, bid managers. Saves: repeated escalations and unsafe "yes" answers. Browser: possible if packaged as static JSON with source dates and scope; server needed for freshness/search across large content. AI: useful for Q&A, but only with strict citation and "not in KB" fallback. Size: M–L. This is the planned next item, but it needs structured fields, not just pages.
5. **Effort, staffing, timeline, assumptions/exclusions, and partner pricing — Rank 5.** Who: delivery lead and partner sales. Saves: rebuilding estimates and avoiding Adobe software pricing. Browser: yes; tie to service lines and rate card. AI: not necessary. Size: M. Procurement needs assumptions/exclusions and staffing plans, not just service bullets.
6. **Go/no-go intake and RFP scoring.** Who: partner sales lead. Saves: time on unwinnable or out-of-scope bids. Browser: yes for a checklist and scoring; server/AI not required. Size: S–M. Capture deadline, client, products, mandatory pass/fail, incumbent risk, and partner certifications.
7. **Security questionnaire answer bank and evidence mapping.** Who: security/compliance lead. Saves: repeated CAIQ/SIG-style answers and inconsistent claims. Browser: static answer bank possible; server needed for current certifications and approvals; AI can retrieve with citations. Size: L.
8. **Procurement-portal exports and post-submission workflow.** Who: bid manager. Saves: reformatting into Excel/Word/PDF and handling clarifications/BAFO. Browser: yes for exports; server needed for shared clarification tracking. AI: optional. Size: M.

## C. What I would change in what is already there

1. **Rename "Not sure" to "Needs triage"** because "Not sure" describes the partner's confidence, not the RFP's state. Procurement does not care that the tool is unsure; the partner needs a queue.
2. **Rename "Use this answer" to "Accept draft"**, **"Edit" to "Edit draft"**, **"Skip" to "Exclude from response"**, and **"Next question" to "Save and next."** "Use this answer" is ambiguous between "insert" and "trust"; "Skip" sounds temporary when it may mean omitted from the submission.
3. **Rename "Add to my statement of work" to "Add these services to SOW"** and show the count, e.g. "Add 4 service lines to SOW." It carries service lines, not the whole answer; the current label overpromises.
4. **Rename "What you have in place" to "Client prerequisites confirmed"** and rename the boxed note **"Ask your client for" to "Client to provide — internal checklist."** "What you have in place" sounds accusatory and ambiguous about whether the partner or client supplied the fact.
5. **Rename "Part of this proposal" to "Covered by this proposal"** and make it explicit whether it is a license the partner sells or work the partner delivers. "Part of" is too vague for a prerequisite split.
6. **Rename "After launch" to "Ongoing monthly services"** because "After launch" could mean hypercare, warranty, or nothing. The SOW generator needs monthly service clarity.
7. **Rename "Your firm" to "Partner-written response"** and **"Adobe services" to "Adobe product answer (draftable)."** "Adobe services" risks sounding like Adobe Consulting Services, which is explicitly out of scope.
8. **Default every drafted Adobe answer to "Draft — review required," not accepted.** Add a visible `Catalog coverage: Full / Partial / No source` badge at the top of the answer. A first-time partner will otherwise read a fluent draft as an approved answer.
9. **Put the direct compliance answer first.** For compliance matrices, the order should be `Compliance`, then `Our approach`, `What we would do`, `Client prerequisites confirmed`, `Ongoing monthly services`, `Adobe sources`. Procurement reads for the compliance token before the narrative.
10. **Change the Excel `Status` values and columns.** Replace generic `Status` with `Answered — draft`, `Answered — partner`, `Unanswered — mandatory`, `Unanswered — desirable`, `Not applicable`; add `Requirement ID`, `Section`, `Mandatory/Desirable`, `Owner`, `Evidence/Source`. Keep `Response` but label it `Draft response — not submitted`.
11. **Banner the internal note: "Internal — excluded from client downloads."** The current boxed note is partner-facing, but under deadline a partner may copy the whole block. Make the exclusion visible, and rename the download **"Questions for your client"** to **"Client input checklist (internal — do not send)."**
12. **Rename "Sources" to "Adobe product sources; effort is partner estimate."** The source list proves Adobe product claims, not the partner's implementation effort, staffing, or timeline. The current label lets a procurement reader over-read the evidence.
13. **Show `Mandatory` badges on extracted questions and let the partner correct them.** If the finder cannot detect mandatory/desirable, the first-time partner will assume the tool did. A manual tag is cheap and prevents a missed mandatory item.
14. **Add an explicit "No catalog source" state.** When a question mentions hosting, security certification, accessibility, uptime, retention, or license packages, the answer should say "Not covered by the current catalog; do not assert" rather than draft from a nearby product page. This is the honest behavior procurement readers reward.
