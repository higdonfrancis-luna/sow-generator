# Response to the three outside reviews of the RFP Builder prototype

Nov 9 2026. Adam ran `REVIEW_PROMPT.md` past Claude Opus 5.5, DeepSeek and Gemini 4 Argon and pasted their answers back (`reviews/`). This document sorts every finding into confirmed, partly, not reproduced and disagree, with the reproduction behind each verdict, then merges their ideas and renames and proposes what to build, in order. Nothing in the prototype or the product was changed for this response.

**How the verification was done.** The three reviews make 53 distinct claims about behaviour (Opus 24 numbered findings with sub-points, DeepSeek 17, Gemini 9; many overlap). Each claim was reproduced against the shipped page, not from a reading of the code: `harness.js` serves the built prototype (`sow/rfp_prototype/site/`, commit `8ad6ba0` plus `doLine` exported) and drives its own JavaScript (the finder, the piles, the file readers, the matcher, the voice rewrite, the note, the downloads, the hand-off). Seven verification agents each took one area and ran the reviewers' exact inputs, or the closest faithful construction where a reviewer gave only a description, plus RFP files made for the purpose (an Excel compliance matrix, a Word table, PDFs with a page break, a running header and two columns, a scanned page, a CSV with a quoted multi-line cell). A skeptic then re-ran every verdict that was not a plain confirmation and a quarter of the confirmed ones. Inputs, raw outputs, fixtures and screenshots are under `verification/` (one folder per area, `c8_check/` for the skeptic, `results.json` for the structured result).

**The count.** 43 claims confirmed as described, 8 partly (reproduced with a material difference), 2 not reproduced. The skeptic upheld 7 of the 11 contested verdicts, adjusted 2 and overturned 2 (one to confirmed, one from not reproduced to partly). The verifiers also found 47 adjacent defects the reviewers missed. Opus, which ran the code, was right on everything it reported except one count (19 ungrammatical lines; there are 30). DeepSeek, which could not run the page, was right in direction on nearly every point and wrong on mechanism in four places. Gemini, which executed the logic, was right on the finder, the spreadsheet reader, the state loss and the leak on the Journey Optimizer connection, and off on several of its named examples.

**The verdict on the prototype.** The reviewers' grades (D+ on real RFP shapes, C+ overall) are fair. The catalog is sound: every drafted sentence is sourced text, and no reviewer found an invented fact. The finder, the piles and the matcher are tuned to the three made-up RFPs and fail on the shapes procurement teams actually send: numbered requirements written as statements, compliance matrices, mandatory tags, sub-questions, lots and appendices. Three things reach the client that should not: partner-only wording, skipped and unreviewed drafts, and wrong-product answers. Work is lost on a refresh, a tab change or a hand-off. None of this needs AI or a server. It needs the finder and the readers rebuilt around real RFP shapes, a few honesty gates in the matcher, client-safe wording for the connection cards, approved-only exports, and the state kept.

## The short version

1. The finder has to read requirements, not just questions. Statements with shall, must, should, will, "ability to", coded IDs such as REQ-101 and FR-3.2, mandatory and desirable tags, lettered sub-questions under a stem, lot and section headings, two-sentence questions, and openers such as "Briefly describe" or "Respondents must describe" are all dropped or glued together today. Spreadsheets have to be read row by row with their columns understood, Word tables by row, PDFs with running headers removed and pages joined.
2. The piles and the matcher have to stop answering the product they see instead of the question asked. Twenty-six ordinary words send product questions to Your firm. A yes/no or hosting question that names a product gets a build plan. Negation is invisible. "AEM" inside "AEM Assets" scores AEM Sites and picks the wrong connection. Not sure rows are drafted, counted and exported.
3. What reaches the client has to be clean. Six connection cards carry partner-only lifecycle and caution wording into Our approach, 30 service lines come out ungrammatical, seven still say partner or Client, and the downloads print skipped and unreviewed drafts in full with status tags and an intro about "the partner".
4. The client has to be told what we need from them. Bank question 2.2 asks for it and the response says nothing, because Nov 9 moved every prerequisite into the partner note. The note also caps at six items (20 prerequisites on 12 connections are shown nowhere), files Adobe's own work and client duties under Part of this proposal, and says "the Client" on a partner surface.
5. Work has to survive. A refresh, the Build tab, Change play, Start over, a pile change and Next question all lose typed answers; Save draft holds nothing of the RFP; the hand-off replaces a saved canvas without asking.

### A8. What the reviewers missed

The verifiers and the skeptic found 47 adjacent defects with the same evidence standard. The ones that change the build:

- **Unnumbered questions on their own lines glue into one** when the previous question is under 80 characters: "Do you support SSO? Do you encrypt data at rest? What is your uptime SLA? Describe your approach to AEM Sites." becomes one question drafted as AEM Sites. The same rule glued "Lot 2", "[M] 2.5", "M1 (Mandatory)", "FR-3.2" and "S1." in the findings above. A two-sentence question on two lines splits the other way, the second half landing in Not sure.
- **Common request openers are not recognised:** "Briefly describe", "Respondents must describe", "Bidders are asked to explain", "In no more than 300 words describe", "Kindly outline", "The vendor should describe" are all dropped.
- **A stem ending in a colon survives alone** while its bullets vanish; upper-case "(A)", "(B)" items are dropped; numbered questions under 12 characters ("1. Why us?") are dropped; "Questions? Contact the procurement office ..." becomes a question.
- **Two-column PDF pages interleave** the columns into one line, so the right column's questions are lost. Legacy .xls is accepted but the mini library cannot parse it, and its internal error reaches the status line. Single-letter Priority and Comply cells (M, D, Y, N, P) look like numbers to the reader, so the column order decides whether a row survives and whether the tags glue onto the exported text.
- **"Do you support ...?" questions inside an RFP that names products get an ongoing-services answer** drawn from the whole RFP's products ("support" triggers the ongoing path), which is why Gemini saw nothing when it pasted one alone.
- **"target" matches "targeting"**, so a CJA audiences question is answered as Target. The ordinary word "target" also sends a schedule question to Adobe services. The work regex sends questions about the vendor's own practices ("Are you SOC 2 Type II certified?", "Describe the training you will provide", "Where will the solution be hosted?") to Adobe services with no draft, so they are tagged as Adobe questions and show No match.
- **The response never carries an availability condition**, not even the ones the SOW prints to the client. The draft reads only the card's how and gain, the line scopes and, for the note, the prerequisites.
- **The six-item cap also truncates the checklist download**, so those 20 prerequisites reach the partner nowhere. The checklist's intro says nothing in it is in the response, which stops being true once a fact is typed. A pile change keeps typed facts but drops everything else.
- **Changing the pile of the open row leaves a stale panel** whose buttons act on the wrong kind of question. The UI says Used and Skip, the exports say Reviewed and Skipped by the partner. At 320px the product's own masthead overflows, not because of the badge.

The full list, with inputs and outputs, is in `verification/results.json` (the `extra` arrays) and the per-area `out_*.json` files.
