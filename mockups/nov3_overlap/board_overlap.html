The CJA play at $150 an hour, Typical, with a data warehouse, cloud storage and a CRM added ($240,000), then CJA data preparation in Experience Platform turned on. A final check of step 27 found that the rule you approved works for one source but not for three: turning a service on lowers the total, and the SOW loses the source work.

Today (step 27)

Data preparation covers the three source services: each shows as covered and adds $0 unless the partner keeps it.

Turning data preparation on: $240,000 to $231,600. The SOW then has no service for the warehouse, the files or the CRM.

Proposed

Every service counts. The canvas flags the overlap on the source service, and the SOW asks how the two differ before it can be issued.

Turning data preparation on: $240,000 to $252,000. Nothing drops out; the partner settles the overlap in the SOW.

Why change it

  • It lowers the total. The three source services are 136 planning hours at Typical; data preparation is an 80-hour placeholder, so turning it on drops $8,400.
  • Data preparation doesn't describe this work. Its scope: "Design the XDM schemas, create the datasets and set up ingestion for the channels CJA will report on, such as web, app, call center and point of sale data." Adobe: "Data Mirror requires relational schemas" and "fields from standard XDM schemas do not automatically merge with similar named fields from relational schemas" (Experience League: Data Mirror overview; CJA, create a connection).
  • It is simpler and steadier. Five of the reviewers' state bugs come from covering and keeping across Build and Services. The overlap review has no hidden state.
  • The tool already works this way for Brand Concierge's question mapping beside the build: both count, and the SOW asks how they differ.

Options

A (recommended): the overlap review, as shown.

B: keep covering, but only cloud storage and the CRM (not the warehouse). It can still lower the total at Small.

C: keep it as built.
