What the second pass changes

  • The $480,000 EDS figure is unsubstantiated, not necessarily excessive. It buys 3,200 modeled hours at $150/hour. It could fit a substantial implementation. It cannot be defended by five service titles without pages, blocks, integrations, migration exceptions, role mix and acceptance criteria. Smaller existing-site work must not inherit a new build.
  • Some offers are too broad for their hours; others repeat the same work. The commerce storefront card can look like a complete implementation at starter-package effort. Conversely, several product combinations charge again for collection, data modeling, reports, content, training or Skills that can be shared.
  • Small, medium and large currently mix three different things: customer scope, uncertain delivery effort and rate assumptions. Each must become a separate input. More monitored prompts cannot automatically double a website build.
  • A citation needs a claim boundary. Separate “Adobe documents this capability,” “a provider advertises this price,” and “we estimate this work will take these hours.” No current catalog price is confirmed by a partner rate card or observed project actuals supplied for this audit.
  • A second model agreeing does not validate a commercial estimate. Independent review catches errors. Comparable delivery estimates and actual engagements are still needed to calibrate the model.

How small, medium and large should work

Choose a bounded scenario, show its quantities, then estimate its work. Each scenario has its own low/high effort assumptions; these are planning ranges, not probability intervals. Partner-entered role rates determine fees. The reference examples below retain $150/hour only to allow comparison with the original tool. It is not an approved rate or a market-rate claim.

The EDS examples are deliberately explicit: Small is 50 final URLs, six blocks and one standard integration; Medium is 250 URLs, 12 blocks and three standard integrations; Large is 1,000 final URLs total across up to three locales, 24 blocks and six named integrations. Ready content and an existing design system are assumed. Commerce and authenticated applications are excluded. The task-level calculations appear in the EDS appendix.

Do not add standalone package prices to assemble a combined play. Form the union of its unique work packages, remove only documented overlap, and recalculate. A percentage discount is not deduplication. The scenario modules later in this report are starting components, not additive ready-made quotes.

Required estimation and commercial fields

For every selected service, capture: existing/new/remediation state; quantity and complexity; delivery owner; included customer work; deliverable and acceptance test; dependencies and entitlement; roles and effort; included PM/QA/review rounds; exclusions; recurring capacity and service window; source and evidence category; estimate owner/date; shared-work identities; and one-time versus recurring billing dates.

Estimated labor fee = sum of unique work-item role hours × partner sell rates. For a fixed-fee proposal, show risk allowance and discount explicitly and verify expected delivery cost separately. Do not silently add PM or QA percentages to hours that already include them.

Delivery contribution = fee − loaded delivery labor − direct third-party/absorbed delivery costs. This is not firm net profit. At an entirely hypothetical $15,600 fixed monthly fee and $90 loaded hourly cost, 104 hours leave $6,240 before other expenses; 130 hours leave $3,900. Automation savings, rework, approval delays and customer execution affect this calculation.

Show setup, recurring fee, service start/end dates and contract value separately. Setup followed by nine operating months in a twelve-month calendar is different from setup plus twelve operating months. Software fees, third-party spend and customer labor remain visible exclusions even when the tool focuses on partner services.

Corrections that affect whether a service can be sold

  • Adobe's current CIF guidance places CIF storefront/Core Components in maintenance mode and advises against new projects. Gate that architecture; distinguish a legitimate headless CIF add-on from a new CIF storefront.
  • Workfront's Coworker documentation states an availability restriction for finance, healthcare and some other sensitive-data organizations. Gate XB14 and verify the specific tenant/integration. This source does not establish a product-wide ban on every Coworker use case.
  • Impact Measurement Engine supports eligible opportunities and needs sufficient data. At least 20 High Potential URLs is a qualification planning criterion; it is not a universal minimum for deploying optimizations. A smaller deployment can work without a native impact report. The approximately two-week description is specific to Recover Content Visibility, not an unconditional report SLA. This refines the earlier audit's overly broad minimum interpretation.
  • Platform availability, authoring paths, native versus custom routes, and included entitlements require explicit gates. A relevant documentation link alone cannot confirm a client's installed configuration.

What makes it ready for the meeting

Executive co-design discussion: correct known errors, make scopes and assumptions visible, show a few bounded scenarios, and ask partners to validate delivery feasibility. Describe it as a partner services scoping prototype with externally researched comparators.

Partner quoting aid: each chosen scope has acceptance criteria and prerequisites, actual partner rates, a delivery lead's reconciled estimate and consistent SOW output. Unknown custom work is identified, not disguised by a number.

Standardized commercial offer: compare estimates with paid-project actuals, rework, margin, customer acceptance and recurring demand. Two independent delivery estimates and two or three pilots can expose major errors; they will not establish an industry-wide median.

Bring partners a reviewable scope packet: one simple installed-base case and one complex case, with quantities, client responsibilities and exclusions. Ask them to estimate role-hours before showing our proposed hours to reduce anchoring. Capture the disagreements by work package. That is the remaining evidence research alone cannot supply.

Executive judgment

No catalog EDS price is validated by the cited product docs. The $480,000 example can be a defensible scoped large replatform, but current page/template/integration quantities are absent, so neither too high nor fair can be established.

Current A01–A05 values are a mechanical 1:2:4 scaling: 800/1,600/3,200 hours, or 120k/240k/$480k at the illustrative $150/h. The source gives one site, but never fixes page, block, interface, language, or approval quantities for each tier. Prompts are not a valid EDS build-size driver.

The large screenshot is 400 person-days at eight hours/day. That is 20 person-months at 160h/month, e.g. five full-time equivalents for four months before utilization/calendar adjustments. The user should be able to see that staffing implication and ask which roles and work require it.

Required branch before estimating

  1. Existing EDS site: scope only incremental readiness, publishing/integration and bounded optimization. No default A01–A05 replatform.
  2. Pilot or partial production rollout: selected pages and reusable blocks, explicit production/nonproduction boundary.
  3. New or migrated site: choose EDS or traditional AEM per page set, inventory the work, then estimate.
  4. Commerce storefront: separate transaction-specific discovery and WBS; cannot silently inherit a marketing-site estimate.

Adobe explicitly supports partial EDS rollout [EDS-S1]. Distinguish AEM Sites entitlement from requiring an AEM author/publish instance [EDS-S7].

Existing-site increment (illustrative planning hypothesis)

For an already working EDS site with access and baseline monitoring, a readiness/publishing-validation work package could use 24–48h for one domain and workflow, 48–80h for two sites/workflows, and 80–144h for three locale workflows with additional approvals. At $150/h that is 3.6–7.2k/7.2–12k/$12–21.6k. This is a candidate replacement boundary for C03, owned by the visibility reviewer; do not add it on top of build work that already includes the same activation. It excludes BV strategy/setup, content production, new blocks, migration and new enterprise analytics.

Findings that change the offer

  1. Existing vs new Concierge must be a branch. F02 launches a new experience. C04 explicitly assumes an existing one and includes source checks, test paths and a handoff test. Defaulting both risks duplicate work. For a new launch, include the technical/source/handoff baseline in F02 and charge only additional BV question mapping under C04. For an existing healthy deployment, omit F02 entirely.
  2. Native automation materially changes the labor story. Adobe documents creating a baseline concierge in about five minutes and a first review/setup exercise in 15–30 minutes. Production readiness is separate: the partner adds content decisions, evaluations, integration validation, rollout and ownership. A multiweek deployment calendar is not itself proof of hundreds of labor hours. [S1, S2, S3]
  3. The new-launch line is missing an explicit WBS. Add readiness/ownership, source audit, use-case boundaries and disclosures, measurement, consent integration validation, accessibility/responsive checks, test corpus and repeated regression, controlled release, rollback/disable procedure, handoff staffing and training. These should be subdeliverables of the scoped launch, not automatic extra fees on top of 640 hours.
  4. Generated answers are not preapproved answers. Describe an experience grounded in customer-approved sources and tested against an agreed evaluation set. The customer retains legal/compliance decisions. Acceptance of sampled behavior cannot certify all future generated responses. Adobe itself tells users to verify out-of-scope behavior through evaluation. [S4]
  5. Concierge technical operations are not the same as FSI answer review. FSI01 currently covers 40 sampled conversations and a report, not ingestion failures, deployment incidents, source correction, technical regression, live-chat staffing or 24-hour support. Those responsibilities need owners. They can remain client-owned or be an optional separately bounded operational package.
  6. Coworker skills must be counted by unique workflow. CW03 and CW-CJA/CW-AEM/CW-FSI, XA25/26/27 and AXD11 can be different implementations of the same billed unit. Put each approved workflow in one register with one owner and one build cost. Prefer adapting available native Skills where they meet the need. A generic skill-authoring package must be reduced or replaced when specialized workflows are selected.
  7. The foundation is shared. CW00 already says it replaces CW01+CW02+CW05. Enforce that in the calculator, not merely in exclusions. Reuse access/context/training between connected products and across plays.
  8. Plugin packaging is optional. CW07 is a sensible distribution/release service when an organization needs an installable package. It is not required for every two-workflow pilot. CW04 configures a supported connector; it does not build an MCP server or product integration. Skills and plugins inherit the connected applications' access controls. [S5, S6]
  9. Specific availability gates are necessary. Coworker Chat currently lists B2B editions of RTCDP, AJO and CJA as forthcoming; Coworker Projects is also forthcoming. Do not sell an autonomous long-running Projects implementation as a current Chat pilot. Adobe onboarding/native integrations belong to Adobe unless explicitly delegated. [S7]
  10. FSI + Workfront Coworker is a direct warning case. Workfront's October 22 documentation says Coworker is not currently available to organizations in finance, healthcare and some other sensitive-data industries. Gate XB14 for that route. This source is specific to Workfront; it does not establish that every Coworker route is unavailable to every FSI customer. Confirm the actual tenant/application before selling CW-FSI. [S8]
  11. RTCDP profile context cannot be an unconditional default. Concierge release notes identify it as limited availability, while the FAQ still describes anonymous use and future profile lookup. XA09 requires tenant-specific written capability confirmation. The diagram must reflect that condition. Similarly, multilingual Concierge is listed as limited availability; do not multiply a default price by language count before availability is established. [S9, S10]
  12. Conversation export has real dependencies. The documented Coworker CSV route needs Data Distiller; Adobe points to a separate Terminal route when it is absent. CW-FSI should specify which route, entitlement, access, storage, retention and reviewer workflow are in scope. Manual FSI01 can use another customer-approved access route; it does not inherently require Coworker. [S11]
  13. Native connections are not repeated builds. XA12 and AXE03 are alternative knowledge-source approaches for the same content. AEM Content AI updates do not require a separately built refresh pipeline. XA11 web placement belongs inside a new launch. XB11 should price Marketo campaign logic and validation, because the underlying Concierge activity flow is native. [S4, S12]
  14. Business outcomes need a comparator. Concierge conversations and purchases can be associated; a higher conversion rate among chat users does not establish that chat caused the difference. Define holdout or staged comparisons where feasible and report uncertainty. This is an audit recommendation, not an Adobe guaranteed outcome.

Estimating method to implement

  1. Separate existing usable assets from missing/new artifacts. Record the readiness evidence and customer prerequisites.
  2. For each artifact, set quantities: source mappings, event types, templates, journeys, dashboard projects, taxonomy schemas, migration batches and integration scenarios.
  3. Estimate work by role and task: discovery/design, configuration/build, QA/UAT, release, handover and project coordination. Include bounded rework and explicit client review rounds.

Specialty agency versus large systems integrator

A specialist's fixed launch kit, offshore-enabled support retainer, US SEO agency offer, and a large SI transformation team are different products. Enterprise client size does not itself determine which delivery model is needed.

  • Specialty agency / Adobe boutique: narrow scope, reusable assets, focused team, limited organizational change. Use explicit deliverables and a capacity-capped retainer; set role rates from the partner's actual approved card.
  • Large SI: architecture, multiple business units, integrations, security/governance, program management and organizational change may dominate effort. Use a staffed work breakdown with role mix, geography, assurance, dependencies and milestones. Request the SI's own rate card. Public federal ceilings cannot replace it.
  • Client-owned delivery: when the client's writers, developers or SEO team executes a task, charge for the agreed advisory/QA contribution only. Do not also quote full partner execution.

NextRow's displayed support packages imply, by simple price ÷ included hours, roughly 119–225 per included hour across the listed tiers. That is a package ratio, not an hourly offer or margin. Included service coverage, overages and staffing matter. This range and the digital-agency survey make $150/hour possible for a worked example, but they do not establish it as the right rate for every capability or Deloitte engagement.

Rate and margin architecture for every play

  1. Qualify installed vs new. Product licensed, provisioned, healthy, existing collection/identity, supported deployment route and customer resources. New implementation is a separate work package; existing customers should not be billed to rebuild it.
  2. Scope each output. Units, complexity, number of integrations, properties, locales, roles, test paths, review rounds, migration volumes, release constraints and client contribution.
  3. Estimate net effort by role. Delivery effort + PM + QA + approval coordination + explicit risk allowance − actual reused or automated effort. Do not apply prompt-count size to an EDS build or Concierge implementation.
  4. Price with the partner's rates. Fee = sum(role effort × approved sell rate), or fixed package fee validated against expected effort and internal delivery cost. Market examples are a reasonableness check, not the calculation engine.
  5. Compute internal profitability separately. Delivery contribution = (net fee − loaded labor − direct third-party costs − absorbed delivery costs) / net fee. Show stress tests for rework, review delay, discounts and utilization. Keep gross contribution, practice operating margin and firm net profit distinct.
  6. Deduplicate by work, not product. Give each content asset, fix, discovery output and reporting deliverable a work-item ID. One owner/cost; several beneficiary products can point to it. SO2 automation activity belongs inside the full SEO program. O01/O02/O03/O04 can inform unique sub-scopes, but must not independently add fees for the same output.
  7. Phase revenue. Show setup, monthly operating term, and service start month. Separate contract value from calendar-year delivery. Delay and paused-retainer terms need partner approval rather than an unconditional free extension.

Verdict and limits

The original model cannot honestly be called a validated rate card. Correct arithmetic, plausible work, and real Adobe documentation do not validate the quantities, hours, rates, total fees, customer demand, or partner margins. A hard audit can make the service architecture and assumptions defensible now. Public research can establish comparable advertised offers and credible delivery requirements. The missing final calibration requires partner delivery estimates and actual engagements; another model's agreement is not a substitute.

The rebuild should therefore produce a bounded scoping and estimating tool with visible evidence levels. It should not claim that all 184 prices have been independently market validated. For lines without usable scope drivers or credible estimates, show “Discovery required” and exclude an invented fee from totals until the partner supplies one.

Evidence levels and required per-line metadata
